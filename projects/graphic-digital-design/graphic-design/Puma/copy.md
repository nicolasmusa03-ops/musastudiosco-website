# Puma Yearbook Graphic

A collaborative graphic with Miami photographer Jose Musa imagining what it would look like if Puma sponsored a high school yearbook — candid portraits, sneakers held up like trophies, and all the chaotic energy of a spread that was never supposed to take itself seriously.

The grid layout does the heavy lifting here: twelve portraits arranged in three tight rows, each photo treated as its own panel but unified by the hand-scrawled annotations layered over them. The doodles — smiley faces, lightning bolts, shout-outs — borrow directly from the language of actual yearbook defacement, giving the branded imagery a texture that feels genuinely personal rather than manufactured. The restrained light blue background keeps the composition grounded without competing with the noise above it.

Typography lands exactly where it needs to: the Puma wordmark anchored at the bottom acts as the punchline, the moment you clock that this is a brand piece. The contrast between the polished logo and the deliberately rough handwriting above it is the whole joke — and the whole point.
