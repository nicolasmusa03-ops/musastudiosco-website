# Coffee

## Tagline
A daily ritual, elevated to a design statement.

## Overview
The concept was simple: make something people would actually want to hang on their wall. Not a coffee brand campaign, not an advertisement — a poster that celebrates the quiet ritual of making coffee without overselling it. The constraint I gave myself was a single color, a single illustration, and a headline that functions as both a command and a permission slip.

## Design Process
Designing with a single color forces a kind of discipline that color freedom never demands. When you remove the ability to use contrast, warmth, or saturation to guide the eye, every decision about scale, weight, and placement becomes critical. Red on white was chosen not just for its visual energy but for its emotional associations — urgency balanced with warmth, which is exactly what a morning cup of coffee is.

The illustration is the emotional center of the piece. I drew the hands loosely — a pour in progress, the cup receiving it — with a line weight that's confident but not mechanical. The linework is gestural enough to feel human but clean enough to read at a glance. The decision to render it as pure outline rather than filled shapes lets the white of the paper breathe through the image, which keeps the illustration from feeling heavy against the large typographic elements surrounding it.

The typography is the system I'm most proud of. "Slow" is set in a flowing italic serif — soft, unhurried, almost languid. "Down" lands in a massive bold sans directly beside it, stopping that softness in its tracks. Then the white space. Then "Drink" in the same bold sans at the bottom, and "Coffee™" returning to the flowing script — but now with a trademark symbol that applies corporate authority to a completely personal, uncapitalizable moment. That ™ is a joke and a philosophy at the same time: we claim ownership of the things we love, even when no one can actually own them.

The small secondary copy — "grab your beans & brew your worries away / start your day off right" — sits quietly to the right of the illustration, subordinated to the compositional drama but present for anyone who slows down enough to notice it. Which, given the poster's subject, feels appropriate.

## Design Principles Applied
- **Single-color discipline** — forced reliance on scale, weight, and placement rather than chromatic contrast
- **Gestural illustration** — loose linework communicates humanity and warmth without sacrificing legibility
- **Typographic personality through contrast** — serif and sans-serif in direct tension create rhythm and wit
- **Humor as design element** — the ™ symbol is a conceptual choice that rewards close reading
