# Born2Create x Studio24

## Tagline
Creative energy, coffee shop warmth.

## Overview
The second Born2Create event shifted gears — same community, different mood. Partnering with Studio24 meant leaning into a more intimate, café-culture atmosphere: casual conversations, new connections, and the kind of creative energy that happens when people actually slow down. The poster had to earn that shift.

## Design Process
The original Born2Create poster spoke in a grunge dialect — high contrast, raw, underground. For the Studio24 collaboration, I needed to evolve the visual language without abandoning it entirely. The answer was warmth. I moved from stark black-and-white to a cream-and-red palette that immediately reads differently — approachable rather than confrontational, analog rather than editorial.

The hand-drawn icon system was the design decision I'm proudest of here. Rather than using photography or stock assets to represent the event's activities — coffee, music, art, DJ, photography — I illustrated each one with a loose, gestural line style that sits somewhere between a sketchbook doodle and a vintage editorial illustration. This choice carried two benefits: it kept the poster feeling handmade and personal, and it gave each activity equal visual weight in a way that a bulleted list or a photo grid never could.

Typography played the tension game. The "BORN2CREATE" headline is massive and confrontational — all caps, heavy weight, completely filling the top of the poster. The "(in collaboration with)" bridge is set in a smaller, parenthetical hand-script, creating a deliberate pause before "STUDIO" hits again in bold display type. That rhythm — loud, quiet, loud — mirrors how a good conversation flows.

The portrait treatment carries the collage DNA from the first poster but in a new colorway. The red duotone on the cut-out photos of the hosts (Gabrielle + Jose) and featured artist integrates them into the poster's palette while the torn, imprecise edges keep everything from feeling too composed. The hosts are identified with their social handles rather than formal titles — a small choice that signals how this community actually operates.

## Design Principles Applied
- **Palette as mood-setter** — swapping monochrome for cream-and-red completely redefines the event's emotional register
- **Illustration over photography** — hand-drawn icons communicate authenticity and give the piece a consistent handmade voice
- **Typographic rhythm** — alternating scale and weight creates visual pacing that mirrors human conversation
- **Collage with intention** — torn edges and duotone portraits tie the new colorway back to the series' established language
