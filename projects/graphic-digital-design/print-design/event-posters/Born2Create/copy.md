# Born2Create

## Tagline
Where the creative underground comes to surface.

## Overview
Born2Create was conceived as a gathering place for Miami's artistic community — a space for artists, photographers, musicians, and makers to exist in the same room and find each other. The poster needed to feel like it came from that world: raw, handmade, and deliberately unpolished.

## Design Process
The brief was the community itself. Born2Create wasn't a corporate event — it was built by and for creatives — so any design language that felt too produced or too clean would have betrayed the event's identity. I leaned into a zine-and-graffiti aesthetic as the foundation: the logotype is hand-lettered with expressive, almost aggressive brushwork, treating the name like a tag rather than a brand.

The black-and-white treatment was a deliberate choice rooted in unification. The three featured creatives — an artist, a photographer, and a DJ — were shot in different contexts with different lighting conditions. Converting everything to high-contrast monochrome collapsed those differences and gave the poster a singular visual voice. Framing each portrait within a Polaroid border extended that intimacy — Polaroids feel personal, immediate, and real in a way that a clean headshot never could.

Hierarchy was handled through scale and weight contrast rather than color. The "Born2Create" logotype dominates the top third; the "Art Basel" event header anchors the middle with tight, compressed serif type; and the sponsor logos, activities, and social handles occupy the bottom as a dense but scannable grid. Every typographic decision pushed against glossy event-design conventions — the scrawled annotations on the Polaroids, the imperfect alignment, the texture bleeding through — because the event deserved a poster that looked like someone who cared made it by hand.

## Design Principles Applied
- **Visual hierarchy through contrast** — scale and weight do the organizational work that color would typically handle
- **Authenticity over polish** — deliberately rough edges signal community rather than commercialism
- **Typographic personality** — the hand-lettered logo carries the entire emotional tone of the poster
- **Unification through monochrome** — a strict black-and-white palette ties together disparate photographic sources
