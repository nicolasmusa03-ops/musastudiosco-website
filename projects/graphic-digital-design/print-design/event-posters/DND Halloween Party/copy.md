# DND Halloween Party

## Tagline
A promotional horror show — designed to get noticed.

## Overview
DND Musica needed more than a party flyer. They were throwing a Halloween event as a vehicle to get in front of more people within the industry — which meant the poster had to do double duty: pull people in on Halloween energy, and leave an impression that outlasted October 31st. I treated it like a B-movie poster, not a nightlife ad.

## Design Process
The vintage exploitation horror aesthetic was the obvious creative direction, but executing it without looking lazy required restraint in the right places. I started with the paper — a warm, aged, off-white base with visible texture and subtle distressing throughout. This is the foundation that every element has to earn its place against. Bright or clean imagery would have broken it immediately; everything needed to feel like it had been photocopied one too many times.

The collage methodology came from zine and cut-and-paste culture. Disembodied eyes peering in from the top, an open screaming mouth bleeding into the bottom corner, a black cat with glowing red eyes at center — these aren't random horror decorations. Each element was chosen and placed to create the feeling of being watched and surrounded without any single image dominating. The result is an unease that creeps rather than shouts.

Color discipline was strict: the aged paper, near-black photographic elements, and selective deep red for type and accents. The red does all the hierarchy work — "DND Halloween" in a distressed bold brush script, the date and time, and the "Open Bar" and "Costumes Required" call-out buttons all read in red. Everything else yields to them. The starburst badge for "21+ Event" is a direct nod to classic movie rating badges — another knowing wink at the genre.

The DND logo sits at the top center as if it were a studio imprint, borrowing from how film production company logos frame vintage posters. It gives the piece a sense of institutional authority that contrasts darkly with the horror imagery below — which is exactly the kind of visual tension a music group trying to be taken seriously needs.

## Design Principles Applied
- **Texture as storytelling** — aged paper and distressed elements establish the era and mood before a single word is read
- **Color reduction for impact** — a near-monochrome palette with strategic red accents makes hierarchy unmissable
- **Collage as atmosphere** — layered horror imagery builds unease through accumulation rather than a single dramatic focal point
- **Genre fluency** — referencing exploitation poster conventions (starburst badges, studio logo placement) signals craft to anyone paying attention
