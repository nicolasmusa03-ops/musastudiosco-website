# Commissions

Commission work is a different discipline than personal work — the measure of success isn't the artist's vision, it's the client's. These pieces represent two distinct briefs: a full family portrait rendered in graphite, and a conceptual magazine cover for DESNUDO. Both required the same fundamental skill as the celebrity series, but applied in service of someone else's moment.

The family portrait demanded compositional control above all. Five subjects — two adults, three children — needed to read as a coherent unit without losing individual presence. The decision to fade the rendering at the edges keeps the eye inside the group, and the coordinated plaid patterns across the figures create visual rhythm that ties the composition together without relying on a background. The DESNUDO cover operated differently: the brief was editorial, so the approach was graphic. The figure's turned pose and the bold masthead typography do the heavy lifting — it's designed to read as a cover at a distance, with the hyperrealism functioning as the payoff when you move closer.

What commission work demands that personal work doesn't is precision without ego. The hand is still there — the value gradients, the rendering of fabric, the attention to individual likeness — but it's subordinated entirely to what the client needs to feel when they see the finished piece.
